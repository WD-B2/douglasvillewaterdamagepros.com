Flood Water Carries More Than Moisture

Water that enters from outside crosses lawns, driveways and storm drains before it reaches your floors. It can carry soil, fuel, bacteria and debris, so we treat it as contaminated until the cleanup is complete. That changes the protective gear, the disposal route and the materials we can save.

The result is a different job from a clean supply-line leak. Porous items such as carpet pad, insulation and soft furnishings are more often removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized before drying begins.

Before you go back in

Do not wade through standing water until power to the area is confirmed off. Leave the electrical panel alone if you have to enter water to reach it.

Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, wash your hands afterward, and keep children and pets out of the rooms.

How Flood Cleanup Proceeds

Pump-out

Standing water is removed in stages so walls and slab are not stressed by sudden pressure changes.

Muck and debris removal

Silt, mud and loose debris come out before anything is dried, because they hold moisture against floors.

Removal of porous materials

Pad, wet insulation and soaked baseboard are taken out and bagged for disposal.

Clean, sanitize and dry

Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated, then air movers and dehumidifiers bring the structure to dry readings.

Documentation matters more here than in a small leak. We record the water line on walls, the rooms involved and the materials removed, which helps whoever is reviewing your claim and helps you decide what to replace.

Insurance and Flood Damage

A standard homeowners policy does not cover flood. Georgia Office of Commissioner of Insurance guidance also notes that water seeping in from outside may not be covered. Federal flood coverage through the National Flood Insurance Program typically has a 30-day waiting period before it takes effect, so the time to ask is before the storm.

Flooding in Douglas County

Douglas County participates in the Community Rating System, and its flood brochure puts the northern and eastern parts of the county in the Sweetwater Creek floodplain and the southeast in the Chattahoochee floodplain. Ask the county whether your address is mapped before you buy a policy, since the answer drives your coverage options.

The September 2009 flood is the benchmark for local residents. The National Weather Service measured 21.03 inches of rain in 24 hours at the Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority, and the Dog River rose 12 feet over the USGS gage. Sweetwater Creek crested at 21.8 feet in 2005 and caused about $6 million in damage, according to the county.

After Flood Cleanup

Most flood jobs continue into structural drying and, in low rooms, basement cleanup. Storm-driven losses that come through the roof or windows are covered under storm water damage cleanup, and if sewage mixed with the floodwater, see sewage backup cleanup.
