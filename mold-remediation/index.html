Why Is There a Musty Smell Where Water Once Leaked?

A musty smell near a past leak usually means materials stayed damp. Drywall, carpet pad and insulation that never dried can support mold growth, even when the surface looks normal.

The EPA says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. If that window passed, the remedy is to find the damp source, remove affected materials and dry the space.

How Mold Remediation Works

The process starts with finding the moisture source, because mold returns if the damp area remains. Crews set containment with plastic sheeting and negative air to keep spores from moving to other rooms, then remove affected drywall, carpet or trim.

Surfaces are cleaned with HEPA vacuums and wiped, and the area is dried to normal readings. Some projects use a third-party tester before and after, which gives an independent record. Ask whether your state licenses mold work, since rules differ by state.

What Not to Do When You Find Mold

Do not spray bleach over large areas, sand the surface or run a fan that blows across it. Those steps spread spores and push moisture deeper into drywall without removing the cause.

Do not paint over a stain either. Paint hides the problem for a few weeks and then the stain returns, because the damp material underneath is still there. Close the door to the room, turn off any fan that pulls air through the area and call for an assessment.

  • Do not disturb a large area before containment is set.
  • Do not cover mold with paint or caulk.
  • Do not skip finding the moisture source, since growth returns without it.

How the Job Ends

Remediation ends when the affected materials are gone, the area is cleaned and the moisture readings are normal. Some owners hire an independent tester to take samples afterward, which gives a record separate from the remediation crew.

Rebuilding follows. New drywall, insulation and trim go in only after the structure is dry, and the moisture source is repaired first so the cycle does not repeat. Keep copies of the readings and photographs for your files.

Insurance and Mold

Many policies limit mold coverage unless it results from a covered water loss, so quick documentation after a leak matters. Photograph the area, keep moisture readings and note when the leak occurred. Ask your carrier how mold is handled in your policy.

Mold in a Humid Georgia Climate

Humid summers keep indoor air moist, so a damp wall cavity dries slowly without dehumidification. Running the air conditioning and using a dehumidifier in the affected area helps, but removal of wet materials is still the sure way to stop growth.

Older buildings in the Douglasville Commercial Historic District, which has structures from the 1880s, have plaster and heavy framing that hold moisture. Those materials need slower drying after a leak.
