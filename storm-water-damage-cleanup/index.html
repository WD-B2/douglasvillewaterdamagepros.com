Storm Water Is Still Inside After the Rain Stops

A storm that blows rain through a damaged roof or window leaves water in insulation, ceilings and walls long after the sky clears. The wet areas are often above you, so the visible damage understates what is trapped behind the surface.

The first priority is to stop more water coming in. Cover damaged roofing with a tarp if you can do it safely, document the damage and call us for removal and drying while a roofer handles the repair.

After the storm

Stay off the roof and stay away from downed power lines. Water near outlets or fixtures means the circuit should be left alone until it is checked.

Photograph the damage before cover-up work and keep receipts for emergency repairs.

What Storm Water Damage Looks Like

Storm losses come in several forms. Roof leaks soak attic insulation and drip into ceilings, broken windows let rain onto flooring and sills, and heavy rain can push water under doors into low rooms.

We trace where the water went with a moisture meter and thermal camera, remove wet insulation and drywall, and dry the structure. Roof and window repairs are left to the roofer or contractor, and we coordinate timing so the interior is protected.

How to Document Storm Damage Safely

Take wide photos from the ground first, then close photos of each stain, wet ceiling and damaged window. Time-stamped photos help show the sequence of events, and a short note of when the rain started and stopped is useful to your adjuster.

Save receipts for tarps, boards and other emergency measures. Keep damaged materials until the adjuster has seen them, unless they pose a hazard. Do not climb onto a wet roof to gather photos, since a drone photo or a roofer inspection is safer.

What to Do Before You Call

  • Stay off the roof and away from downed lines.
  • Tarp a damaged roof from a safe position and photograph it.
  • Keep outlets near wet areas off.
  • Save receipts for emergency measures.

Reference Standards for Storm Losses

Storm intrusion is dried to the published ANSI/IICRC S500 approach, with EPA guidance of 24 to 48 hours as the outer limit for wet materials. Georgia insurance guidance adds a first step, which is to tarp a damaged roof and photograph the damage.

Insurance for Storm Damage

Georgia insurance guidance says to cover a damaged roof with a tarp and document the damage with photos. Wind-driven rain through a damaged opening is typically handled differently from flood water entering at ground level. Ask your carrier which category your loss falls into.

Storms and Douglas County

Douglas County sits in two mapped floodplains, so a storm can bring both roof leaks and ground-level water. The county flood brochure places the north and east in the Sweetwater Creek floodplain and the southeast in the Chattahoochee floodplain, and the answer to which water caused the loss changes your coverage.

The National Weather Service recorded 21.03 inches of rain in 24 hours at the Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority in September 2009. That event is a reminder to check gutters, downspouts and attic insulation before storm season, not after.
