Water Damage Services in Douglasville, GA

Pick the problem that matches yours. Each page explains the signs, the process and what shapes the scope.

Written by the Douglasville Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 14, 2026.

Whatever the source, a water damage job moves through the same stages, and knowing the order helps you ask better questions when you call, because each service below adds a few steps that depend on the water, the materials and the building.

Most water damage jobs follow the same arc. The source is stopped, standing water is removed, the structure is dried with measured readings and the damaged materials are repaired. The pages below cover the sources and surfaces that change those steps.

Not sure which page fits? Start with water extraction if water is standing, or structural drying if the surface is dry but the house feels damp. You can also call (943) 469-0138 and describe it.

Pipes and plumbing

  • Burst Pipe Water Damage. Cleanup and drying after a split supply line.
  • Frozen Pipe Water Damage. Thaw leaks in attics, walls and crawl spaces.
  • Water Heater Leak Cleanup. Tank failures in closets, garages and attics.
  • Slab Leak Water Damage. Drying floors and walls after an under-slab leak.

Appliances and HVAC

  • Appliance Leak Water Damage. Washer, dishwasher and fridge line leaks.
  • AC Leak Water Damage. Condensate overflow in ceilings and closets.
  • Kitchen Water Damage. Cabinet bases, floors and walls dried in place.

Weather and building envelope

  • Ceiling Water Damage and Roof Leaks. Controlled drainage and drying for stained ceilings.
  • Storm Water Damage. Roof and window intrusion after storms.
  • Flood Damage Cleanup. Storm and creek water removed, cleaned and dried.
  • Basement Water Damage. Pump-out, drying and source notes for lower levels.
  • Crawl Space Water Damage. Pump-out and drying under the house.

Water removal and drying

  • Water Extraction. Pumps and wands pull standing water out fast.
  • Structural Drying and Dehumidification. Measured drying of walls, floors and framing.
  • Sewage Backup Cleanup. Protective-gear cleanup of contaminated water.

Floors and walls

  • Hardwood Floor Water Damage. Floor drying that saves boards when possible.
  • Carpet Water Damage. Extraction and drying of carpet and pad.
  • Wall and Drywall Water Damage. Wall cavity drying, flood cuts and patching.

Commercial

  • Commercial Water Damage. Offices, retail and warehouses, after hours if needed.

Secondary service

  • Mold Remediation. Containment and removal after moisture problems.

What to Do Before Help Arrives

  • Stop the water source if you can do it safely.
  • Keep people out of rooms where water meets electrical equipment.
  • Photograph the damage before anything moves.
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Procedures behind these services follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage and ANSI/IICRC S520 for mold, used as references only.
