Water Is Moving Into Your Walls While You Read This

Standing water does not stay put. It wicks upward into drywall, slips under baseboards and drops through gaps toward the subfloor, so the first hour matters more than the second day. Every inch of depth you remove early is moisture the drying equipment never has to chase.

If the water came from a supply line, a failed hose or a heavy storm, the extraction crew works the same way. We remove the bulk water first, then check what the water reached. That order keeps small losses small.

Do these before the crew arrives

Stop the source if you can reach it safely. Close the shutoff valve at the fixture or the main, and keep people and pets out of the wet area.

Leave the breaker alone if you would have to stand in water to reach it. Water and live circuits are a real danger, so wait for guidance on the call.

How Water Extraction Works

Crews match the tool to the depth. A submersible pump handles several inches in a basement or garage, a weighted extraction wand pulls water out of carpet and pad, and a squeegee wand clears hard floors. Household wet vacs are too small for this job and can leave a wet pad behind.

Once the surface water is gone, the crew uses a moisture meter and a thermal camera to map where water went. Wet readings behind a baseboard or under a cabinet kick are common even when the carpet looks nearly dry. That map decides where drying equipment goes next.

Stop and size up

We ask where the water started, how long it has been standing and whether it is clean supply water or something else. That tells the crew what to bring.

Pump and extract

Bulk water comes out first with pumps and extraction wands, working from the lowest point toward the doorways.

Pull what cannot dry

Soaked carpet pad and loose baseboard are often removed on the spot so the floor and wall base can breathe.

Map and hand off

Moisture readings are recorded before drying equipment starts, so progress can be measured against a starting point.

What Changes the Cost of Water Extraction

Extraction is priced by the conditions of the loss, not by a flat number. The table below shows what moves the scope up or down, which is also what we ask about on the phone.

Factors that shape a water extraction scope
FactorWhy it matters
Depth and area of water.More depth means pumps and longer run time. A thin film across a large room still needs wand passes over every square foot.
Floor type.Carpet and pad hold far more water than tile. Hardwood and laminate need fast attention before they cup or swell.
Water source.Clean supply water is handled differently from groundwater or a drain backup, which changes protective gear and disposal.
Hours since the loss.Water that has sat overnight has usually reached the wall base, which adds drying steps after extraction.
Access to the area.Stairs, tight crawl spaces and finished basements affect how equipment is staged and how long the work takes.

What to Do Before Help Arrives

  • Stop the source and keep everyone out of water that touches outlets or appliances.
  • Lift small items and furniture legs off wet floors.
  • Photograph the water and the source before anything moves.
  • Leave household vacuums and heaters out of standing water.

How Extraction Is Measured

Published practice for water loss work is set out in ANSI/IICRC S500. It treats extraction, moisture mapping and drying as one measured sequence, which is why we log readings before and after pumping. We follow that published standard as a reference and do not claim firm-level certification.

Insurance and Water Extraction

Many homeowners policies respond to sudden, accidental water such as a burst supply line or an overflowing appliance, while water that seeps in from outside is treated differently. Take photos before anything moves and keep the receipts for any emergency work. Your adjuster makes the coverage decision, and we document the scope so the claim file is clear.

Water Extraction Around Douglasville

Douglas County sits in two floodplains. Its flood brochure places the northern and eastern areas in the Sweetwater Creek floodplain and the southeast in the Chattahoochee floodplain. If your street is in one of those areas, ask your carrier whether water entering from outside is covered, because that answer changes how you document the loss.

The National Weather Service recorded 21.03 inches of rain in 24 hours at the Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority on Sept 20 to 21, 2009. That number is a useful reminder that saturated ground pushes water into low rooms, so basements and garages deserve a look after any long storm.

After the Water Is Out

Extraction removes liquid water, not the moisture inside framing and flooring. Most losses move straight into structural drying and dehumidification so readings come back to a safe level. If carpet or pad took the hit, see how we handle carpet water damage, and if the source was a plumbing failure, burst pipe repairs and cleanup covers the next steps.
