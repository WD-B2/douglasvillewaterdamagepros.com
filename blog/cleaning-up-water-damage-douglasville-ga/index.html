Cleaning Up Water Damage: What You Can Do Yourself

Some water cleanup is safe for homeowners and some is not. This guide separates the two so you can act quickly without making the damage worse.

Written by the Douglasville Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 14, 2026.

Small spills, short-lived leaks and clean water on hard floors can often be handled at home. Larger losses, contaminated water and wet walls call for equipment you do not own. The goal is to act fast on what you can, and to know when to hand off the rest.

Before You Touch Anything

Check electrical safety. If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out and leave the breaker alone. Turn off the water source, and wear gloves and boots if the water might be dirty.

Decide what kind of water you have. Clean water from a supply line is the best case. Water from a drain, toilet or outside is contaminated, and cleanup should be handled by trained crews.

Safe Steps for Clean Water

  1. Remove standing water with towels, a mop or a wet vacuum made for water.
  2. Lift furniture and rugs off wet floors, and place foil under furniture legs.
  3. Open cabinet doors and remove drawers to let air reach the wet areas.
  4. Run the air conditioner and a dehumidifier. Keep windows closed in humid weather.
  5. Take photos and write down what you did and when.

Speed matters because moisture moves into drywall, baseboards and subfloor within hours. EPA guidance says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.

What Not to Do

Do not use a household vacuum on standing water. Do not use a heater or hair dryer on wet walls, and do not turn on ceiling fans that hang under a stained ceiling. Do not paint over a stain or seal a damp floor.

Do not leave wet carpet in place for days. If the pad is soaked, the carpet may be saved but the pad rarely is, and both need to be dealt with early.

Drying Furniture, Books and Belongings

Solid wood furniture can often be dried and kept. Move it to a dry room, wipe it down and allow air to circulate. Upholstered pieces are harder, because the padding holds water and may stay damp for days.

Paper and books should be separated and dried flat, with towels between wet pages. Photographs can be frozen to pause damage until you have time. Electronics that got wet should be unplugged and checked by a technician before use.

Clean Up After the Water Is Gone

Once surfaces are dry, wipe hard surfaces with a mild detergent. Launder washable fabrics in hot water if the label allows. Discard items that cannot be cleaned, particularly porous items that sat in dirty water.

Check the floor after a week for new odors, soft spots or staining. A musty smell that appears later points to moisture you missed, and a moisture meter is the best way to find it.

A Simple Timeline for the First Two Days

  • Hour one: stop the source, stay safe and photograph the damage.
  • Hours two to six: remove standing water and lift items off wet floors.
  • Day one: start dehumidification, call your carrier and call a crew if walls or carpet are wet.
  • Day two: check readings, remove soaked pad or insulation and keep the house closed.

This outline is general. A clean spill on tile moves faster than a leak inside a wall, and a crew can tell you what applies to your home.

Signs You Missed Something

A musty smell a few days later, paint that bubbles, baseboards that pull away or a floor that feels slightly soft all point to moisture that is still in the structure. A moisture meter can confirm it quickly, and a thermal camera can show the wet path behind a wall.

If any of these show up, treat it as an active loss and begin drying again. Waiting turns a small repair into a larger one.

EPA guidance to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours is the reference for the timeline above, and ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional drying.

Cleanup in Douglasville Conditions

Georgia humidity makes drying harder. Outdoor air can be as damp as the air inside, so opening windows often slows drying instead of helping it. Keep the house closed and use dehumidification instead.

If your basement or crawl space took on water during a storm, remember that Douglas County includes mapped floodplains for Sweetwater Creek and the Chattahoochee. Groundwater and flood water are contaminated, so treat them as a job for trained crews.
